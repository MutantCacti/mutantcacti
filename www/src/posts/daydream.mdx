This piece was an experiment, loosely based on a poem of the same title I wrote in early 2024. There is a version of this song with vocals, some spoken, some sung. I've opted to publish the instrumental here as a means of preserving my dignity. 

My second-year Electronic Music course valued experimentation---we studied examples like Jonathan Harvey's [Mortuos Plango, Vivos Voco](https://youtu.be/nfqub2bfQq0?si=hSCgswyHkGZX1S6U) and the Beatles' [A Day In The Life](https://youtu.be/usNsCeOV4GM?si=UFdIWzkULPSOF-8a), both tracks that influenced me towards trust in fewer, mathematically meaningful sounds that interplay.

This was my first experience with EM production skills like sampling, physically based synthesis, and [acousmatic listening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acousmatic_sound) (For a great example, see The Avalanches' [Frontier Psychiatrist](https://youtu.be/qLrnkK2YEcE?si=hS_ZfpWbWr8T_K9s)). The "ripping" sounds that constitute the midtones of Daydream are all taken from samples of me blowing into my phone microphone, plus some liberal transformation of speech into garbled, glitchy jitter.

Daydream is about the struggle between conscious and subconscious thought, about the feeling of being trapped in a physical medium whose chemical and hormonal variations can have disconcerting effects on the psyche and how isolating that can be. It's not a happy song. It at least makes the effort to be angry while it's wallowing in self-pity.
